Summary Dr Mayue Shi is a biomedical electronics scientist. Currently, he is working as Postdoctoral Researcher in Digital Health at the Institute of Biomedical Engineering, University of Oxford. He is also an Honorary Researcher at John Radcliffe Hospital, part of NHS Oxford University Hospitals. Before this, he was a Research Associate at Imperial College London, working on soft sensors and wearable biomedical devices for healthcare.

Publications

  • 2024
    Multiple Physiological and Behavioural Parameters Identification for Dietary Monitoring Using Wearable Sensors: A Study Protocol
    medRxiv
    This protocol paper describes a study investigating the physiological responses to energy intake utilising a customised wearable multi-sensor band, which is specifically designed to monitor multiple physiological and behavioural responses related to eating and digestion events for dietary monitoring.
  • 2024
    High-Performance Vision-Based Tactile Sensing Enhanced by Microstructures and Lightweight CNN
    arXiv
    Tactile sensing is critical in advanced interactive systems by emulating the human sense of touch to detect stimuli. This paper presents a comprehensive approach combining a novel microstructure-based sensor design and efficient image processing, demonstrating that carefully engineered microstructures can significantly enhance performance while reducing computational load.
  • 2021
    Stretchable Piezoelectric Tensile Sensor Patterned via Ultraviolet Laser Cutting
    2021 IEEE 20th International Conference on Micro and Nanotechnology for Power Generation and Energy Conversion Applications (PowerMEMS)
    This paper reports a piezoelectric dynamic tensile sensor comprising a metalized polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) film patterned with fractal curves and encapsulated in polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS). UV laser cutting provides a simple, fast and cheap fabrication process for stretchable piezoelectric devices including low-power sensors and wearable energy harvesters.
  • 2021
    A comparative review of artificial muscles for microsystem applications
    Microsystems & Nanoengineering
    This paper aims to provide a comparative review of recent advances in artificial muscle based on various operating mechanisms. The advantages and limitations of each operating mechanism are analyzed and compared. According to the unique application requirements and electrical and mechanical properties of the muscle types, we suggest suitable artificial muscle mechanisms for specific microsystem applications.
  • 2020
    Piezoelectric wind velocity sensor based on the variation of galloping frequency with drag force
    Applied Physics Letters
    We demonstrate a miniature energy harvesting wind velocity sensor of simple, low-cost construction, based on a single-degree-of-freedom galloping structure. We have observed a negative correlation between the wind velocity and the vibration frequency, and we demonstrate that this relationship can be used to detect wind velocity directly with useful accuracy.
  • 2017
    Self-powered wireless smart patch for healthcare monitoring
    Nano Energy
    Wearable electronics provides an opportunity for everyone to own personal healthcare systems. Here we present a self-powered smart patch with wireless transmission ability based on triboelectric effect and electrostatic induction to monitor the temperature and motion status of individuals. The wireless transmission efficiency achieved 26.6% with a 16 cm² receiver while the distance is 1 cm, which shows remarkable capability of near-field wireless transmission.
  • 2016
    Self-Powered Analogue Smart Skin
    ACS Nano
    The progress of smart skin technology presents unprecedented opportunities for artificial intelligence. Here, we present a self-powered analogue smart skin for detecting contact location and velocity of the object, based on a single-electrode contact electrification effect and planar electrostatic induction. The sensitivity of this smart skin is remarkable, which can even perceive the perturbation of a honey bee.
