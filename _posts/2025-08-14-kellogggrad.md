The past Saturday was a graduation day at Oxford. Over the past year I served as College Advisor at Kellogg College, and offering a bit of advice to students. Honestly, I was just as new as our students. We learned and grew together.

On Sunday I met in London with a professor I’ve known for more than ten years. Our chat took me back to my student days. I’ve been lucky to have teachers and mentors from many cultures, who gave me not just knowledge but opportunities and recognition, and helped me see a bigger world. That meant a lot when I was younger. I’ve always felt very lucky to meet so many remarkable people, and humble, because I see myself as a survivor.

As work and life move on, external recognition matters less. I learn to listen to my inner voice. Meanwhile, my university roles give me a valuable chance to share what I’ve learned in my experiences, and encourage younger engineers and researchers who are where I once was. That’s a joy of the job.

Congrats to the new graduates.
